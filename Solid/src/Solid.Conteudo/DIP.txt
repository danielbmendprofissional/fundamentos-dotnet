===================================== DEPENDENCY INVERSION PRINCIPLE (DIP) =====================================

The DIP states that high-level modules should not depend on low-level modules, but rather on abstractions.
Abstractions must not depend on details; instead, details should depend on abstractions.


- Benefits: Decoupling (makes it easier to swap implementations without impacting the code) and improved 
testability.

- Bad pratice example: A high-level module that directly depends on a concrete low-level class.

Instead of relying on a concrete class, we depend on abstractions—contracts (interfaces).
For example, a controller calling a service through its interface.

The contract remains fixed, regardless of what happens under the hood. Even if the implementation changes, as 
long as the interface stays the same, the system continues to work.

This approach always helps with code maintenance and evolution.
